## 1. What this agreement is about

The Artist lists original artwork on FutureBox, where members of the app can
buy it as album art for their own songs. The Studio provides the place, the
payment system and the customers; the Artist provides the work.

This is not employment and it is not a partnership. The Artist is an
independent contractor and carries their own tax and expenses.

Nothing here obliges the Studio to sell any number of works, and nothing
obliges the Artist to supply any number of works.

## 2. Being let in

The Studio approves every artist by hand before their work appears on the
platform. The Studio may refuse an application without giving a reason, and
may suspend an artist's access if this agreement is broken.

## 3. What the Artist warrants

For every work they upload, the Artist warrants that:

1. **it is their own original work**, made by hand, and that they have the
   full right to sell it;
2. it **infringes nobody else's copyright, trademark or image rights** —
   including those of any person whose face or name appears in it;
3. it was **not generated mainly by a computer or an AI model**, unless that
   was disclosed to the Studio in writing beforehand and accepted;
4. it has **not already been sold, licensed or exclusively promised**
   elsewhere.

If a claim is brought against the Studio because one of these warranties was
untrue, the Artist indemnifies the Studio against it.

## 4. Unique, and sold once

This is the rule the whole room is built on, and it is the most important
clause in this document.

**Every work is sold exactly once.** Once a work has sold on FutureBox:

- it leaves the gallery and nobody else can buy it;
- the Artist may **not sell, license, offer as album art, or issue as a
  print or an NFT, the same work again** — not on FutureBox and not anywhere
  else;
- the Artist may still **show** it in a portfolio or on social media as work
  they made, crediting the buyer's song where that is appropriate.

While a work is for sale on FutureBox, the Artist may not offer it for sale
elsewhere at the same time. To sell it elsewhere, it must first be taken off
FutureBox.

**What the buyer gets.** A buyer receives a perpetual, worldwide, exclusive
licence to use the work as the cover of their own music: in the app, on
streaming services, on social media, on a poster or a sleeve, and in
advertising for that music. The copyright itself stays with the Artist. The
buyer may not resell or re-license the work as an artwork in its own right.

## 5. Prices and commissions

**Off the wall it is an auction.** The Artist sets the opening bid, with a
floor of **R200** per work. From there buyers bid against each other, and the
**highest bid when the clock stops takes the work**.

The clock runs for **36 hours** from the moment the work is hung. Each new bid
must beat the last by at least **R20**. A bid in the final five minutes pushes
the clock out by five, so a work is not won on a fast connection.

The Artist may **not bid on their own work**, and may not ask anybody else to
do so on their behalf. It is the one rule without which an auction means
nothing, and the app refuses it too.

What is paid is the winning bid, not the opening one. The profit in clause 6
is computed on that amount.

**Commissions.** A buyer may ask an artist for a one-off work for a
particular song. The app lets the buyer say only two things — *"I want unique
art"* and which of their own songs it is for. The Artist answers with a price
and one of four windows: **2 days, 4 days, 6 days or 2 weeks**. The guide
price for a commission is **R500**.

The clock starts when the buyer presses *accept*, not when the price was
offered. A commission is delivered to that one buyer and to nobody else, and
clause 4 applies to it exactly as to any other work.

**No conversation off the platform.** The app's messages are deliberately
buttons only: there is nowhere to type. The Artist undertakes not to exchange
contact details with a buyer in order to do a sale outside FutureBox. This is
the one thing this agreement treats as a material breach.

## 6. How the money is split

The card fee comes off the **winning bid** first. What is left is the
**profit**, and the profit is split **70% to the Artist and 30% to the
Studio**.

**The buy-in does not count here.** To be allowed to bid at all, a buyer pays
the Studio a once-off **R50**. It is a door fee, there to stop somebody who is
not serious from running a price up and walking away. It is not part of the
price of a work, it is not deducted from a winning bid, and **the Artist
receives none of it**. The Artist is paid on the winning bid and on nothing
else.

The table below shows a few amounts. It is not a price list — it is what comes
out at different winning bids.

This is deliberately on the profit and not on the sticker price. A "70%"
computed on the sticker price pays the Artist 70% of money that never
arrived — on a R200 piece that is R6.30 of somebody else's money, every time.

The card fee is **3.5% of the payment plus R2**. That is what the payment
provider charges and the Studio keeps none of it.

The Artist's share is rounded down and the remaining cent stays with the
Studio. Not because the Studio deserves it — because the three figures have to
add up to exactly what was paid, and somebody has to carry the half cent. The
sum is done in whole cents so that it is only that half cent, and not a whole
one lost to a computer's rounding.

## 7. Payment, and the invoice

**When.** The Studio pays **monthly, at the end of each calendar month**, for
every sale completed in that month. A sale is complete when the winner has paid
and — for a commission — the work has been delivered. An auction that has
ended but not been paid for is not a sale and is not paid out.

A sale completed on the last day of a month falls in that month. A refund or
a reversed payment is deducted from the following month's payout.

**How.** Electronic transfer to the bank account above. The Artist is
responsible for telling the Studio in writing, before the end of the month,
if those details change.

**The invoice.** **After** the Artist has received the money, they must send
the Studio an invoice for the amount received within **seven (7) days**. The
invoice must carry:

- the Artist's name, address and ID or registration number;
- an invoice number and the date;
- the month it is for;
- the works sold, and the amount per work;
- the total, which must equal exactly what was transferred;
- the VAT number and the VAT amount **if** the Artist is VAT registered.

The Studio sends a statement with every payment showing the same rows,
produced by the app itself, so the invoice can be drawn up against it.

An artist who does not send invoices cannot be paid further — the Studio has
to be able to prove the expense.

**VAT.** The Studio is not the Artist's agent for VAT purposes. If the Artist
is VAT registered, the amounts above are inclusive of VAT unless otherwise
agreed, and the Artist handles their own VAT returns. Each party carries its
own income tax.

## 8. Credit

The Artist's name and the work's title travel with the song — into the
buyer's channel, and into the Live room under the song's own name. The Studio
undertakes not to remove the credit. The Artist grants the Studio permission
to use their name and work to display and market the gallery itself.

## 9. Ending it

Either party may end this agreement on **thirty (30) days'** written notice.

What has already sold stays sold: clause 4 and the buyer's licence hold
permanently, including after this agreement ends. Money earned before the end
is still paid at the end of that calendar month, against an invoice as set
out in clause 7.

The Studio may remove a work from the gallery immediately if there is a
reasonable claim that it infringes somebody else's right, and will not pay
out for it until the matter is resolved.

## 10. The rest

**Confidentiality.** Neither party discloses the other's business or customer
details.

**Whole agreement.** This document is the whole agreement between the parties
on this subject and replaces anything discussed before it. A change counts
only if both parties sign it in writing.

**Law.** The law of the Republic of South Africa applies. The parties will
try between themselves first, then mediation, and only then a court.
