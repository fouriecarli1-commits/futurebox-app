# Singer agreement (voice model) — FUTUREBOXSTUDIO (Pty) Ltd

> **When this one applies, and when it does not.** If the intention is to use singers
> for **pronunciation** rather than to give their voices to members, this is the wrong
> document — use `docs/SINGER-PRONUNCIATION.md`, which is far shorter and far easier to
> ask for. This one applies only where a voice model is made and members can sing in it.
>
> **Read this first.** This document was drafted by building the app, not by a
> lawyer. It says exactly what the app does and what Carli undertakes to do, in
> plain language, and it is an honest starting point — but have a lawyer look at
> it **before the first singer signs**. The reason is sharper here than in the
> artist agreement: a voice is **special personal information** under POPIA, and
> the Act sets stricter requirements for consent to it than for anything else
> this app handles.
>
> The Afrikaans version is `docs/SANGER-OOREENKOMS.md`. The two say the same
> thing; if they ever differ, the one both parties signed governs.
>
> **Why this is not part of the artist agreement.** That one's whole spine is
> clause 4 — *"Unique, and sold once"*. A voice model is the opposite: it is used
> over and over, by many people, for an indefinite time. And an illustrator who
> will never give a voice should not have to sign clauses about biometric data.
>
> The recording side of this — what to record, for how long, in what order — is
> in `docs/STEMBIBLIOTEEK.md`.

## 1. What this agreement is about

The Singer comes and sings, the Studio records it, and from those recordings a
**voice model** is made: an AI model that can make another person's singing sound
like the Singer's voice. Members of FutureBox can then put their own singing
through that model.

This is not an employment agreement and not a partnership. The Singer is an
independent contractor.

Nothing here obliges the Studio ever to use or publish the model, and nothing
obliges the Singer to make further recordings.

## 2. Consent under POPIA

The Singer hereby gives **express written consent** for the Studio to process his
or her voice for the purpose in clause 1.

The Singer understands:

- A voice is **special personal information** under the Protection of Personal
  Information Act 4 of 2013 — a higher category than a name or an address,
  because a voice identifies a person.
- The Studio is the **responsible party**. The voice also goes to a processor
  outside South Africa — Kits.AI — which trains and stores the model; this is
  named on `/privacy`.
- The Singer may ask **what is held**, may have an error corrected, and may use
  clause 6 to stop.
- Consent is given freely and is not a condition of anything else.

**What is held:** the raw recordings, the voice model, and this agreement. The
recordings and the agreement are kept for at least three years, or as long as the
model is in use, whichever is longer.

## 3. What the Studio may do with the model

The Singer grants the Studio a **non-exclusive** licence (unless clause 8 says
otherwise) to:

1. make the voice model available inside FutureBox so that members can put their
   own singing through it;
2. show the Singer's name beside it, as in clause 7;
3. use a demonstration clip of the voice to show what the feature does;
4. retrain the model from the same recordings, at a different service, if the
   Studio's technical arrangement changes. **The model is a version; the
   recordings are the asset.**

## 4. What the Studio may NOT do

This is the clause the Singer should read most closely.

The Studio will not:

1. use the voice model, or let it be used, to make **spoken words** that purport
   to have the Singer say something — not an advertisement, not a news report,
   not a political message, nothing. The licence in clause 3 is for **singing**
   inside the app;
2. sell, rent or transfer the model or the recordings to a third party as part of
   a business transaction without the Singer's prior written agreement;
3. use the model for content the app's own rules forbid — and those rules are on
   `/terms` and are enforced by the app itself;
4. make a model of anyone under eighteen. The Singer warrants in clause 5 that he
   or she is eighteen or older, and the Studio will not make or hold a voice model
   of a child, with or without a parent's consent.

## 5. What the Singer warrants

The Singer warrants that:

1. it is his or her **own voice** being recorded;
2. he or she is **eighteen years or older**;
3. nobody else holds a right in that voice that this agreement would breach — no
   record deal, no management agreement, no exclusivity at another service. **If
   there is a label or a manager, this one must be checked before the session**,
   because it is the only warranty here that a third party can come and enforce;
4. the songs sung during the session do not infringe somebody else's copyright in
   a way that makes the recording unusable. For the training itself it does not
   matter what is sung — the model learns the voice and not the song — but a
   recording that has to be thrown away is a session that has to be thrown away.

## 6. Stopping, and what happens then

This is the hardest clause in this document, and it is in plain language because
the Singer has to understand it **before** signing and not after.

The Singer may give written notice at any time that he or she wants to stop. Then:

1. **Further use stops.** The Studio removes the model from the app within
   **thirty days**, so that nobody can make new songs with it.
2. **The recordings and the model are deleted** within those thirty days, except
   where the law requires something to be kept.
3. **Songs already made remain.** This is the part that cannot be undone. A member
   who made a song with that voice has already downloaded it, released it, has it
   playing on a platform. The Studio cannot unmake that and does not promise it
   can.
4. **What has been paid stays paid** and nothing further is owed.

The Studio may also stop, on thirty days' written notice, and the same applies.
